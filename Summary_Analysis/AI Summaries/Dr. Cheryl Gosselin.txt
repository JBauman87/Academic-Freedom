Between late 2024 and early 2025, Bishop’s University in Sherbrooke, Quebec, became the center of a significant controversy involving allegations of discriminatory behavior and the use of derogatory language by a tenured sociology professor, Dr. Cheryl Gosselin. According to reports from CTV News and an investigation by the student newspaper, *The Campus*, several students have come forward to allege that Dr. Gosselin used racial and homophobic slurs and promoted harmful stereotypes in her classroom for years, while the university administration was criticized for a perceived lack of transparency and action.

The controversy reached a peak following a Fall 2024 course titled "Gender and Sociology" (SOC129). Students in the class reported that on November 12, 2024, Dr. Gosselin used a homophobic slur—referred to as the "F-slur"—multiple times. Student Allister Coursol and others noted that while the professor was quoting from a 2022 academic article by Joel Mittleman regarding homophobic bullying, she continued to repeat the slur several times to prompt classroom discussion. According to student accounts, the repeated use of the word caused an "audible gasp" in the room. When student Aaliyah Wilburn attempted to challenge the use of the language, she alleged that Dr. Gosselin "blew her off," making her feel "dumb" for speaking up. Gosselin reportedly defended her actions by stating the slur was not a word she used on a daily basis and argued that such language needed to be "destigmatized."

The allegations against Dr. Gosselin extend beyond this single incident. Sociology student Malik Kessouagni, who had taken three classes with the professor, alleged that she demonstrated clear biases against Black women and engaged in racial stereotyping. He described an instance where Dr. Gosselin stared directly at him and another Black student while discussing stereotypes regarding Black male anatomy. Other students, including Marie-May Lamothe, alleged that Gosselin freely used the "N-word" during lectures and misgendered non-binary authors. Furthermore, students accused the professor of making Islamophobic remarks, specifically claiming that Gosselin suggested women who wear the hijab "slut shame" Caucasian women.

Historical accounts suggest these issues were not new. Reviews on RateMyProfessor dating back to 2006 and 2015 indicate that students had previously raised concerns about discriminatory language. In one instance, a student from 2015 reported that Gosselin dismissed concerns about her language by claiming it was simply "the language of the time." Another student, Markayza Mitchell, reported a Winter 2023 incident where Gosselin allegedly dismissed her concerns regarding a lack of Indigenous representation in a "Quebec Society" course, eventually telling the student she could leave the class if she did not like the perspective being taught. Mitchell ultimately dropped the course, which she stated delayed her graduation.

The student body’s reaction to these events was one of frustration with the university’s formal complaint processes. Aaliyah Wilburn reported the Fall 2024 incident to the university’s Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) department but felt the administration was looking for "reasons and excuses" for the professor’s behavior rather than holding her accountable. Similarly, student Rory Point-Du-Jour expressed that Gosselin’s unapologetic attitude—characterized as "you guys kind of just have to deal with it"—created an uncomfortable learning environment and set a poor example for other students.

Bishop’s University initially responded to media inquiries by citing privacy laws and a commitment to due process. Communications manager Sonia Patenaude and Vice-Principal Academics Andrew Webster emphasized that the university could not disclose details regarding individual personnel files or confidential investigations. However, following the publication of the CTV News report in January 2025, the university took visible steps. An internal memo from Principal Sébastien Lebel-Grenier acknowledged that the media reports were "challenging and emotionally straining" for the community. Most notably, students confirmed that by the third week of January 2025, Dr. Gosselin’s Winter semester classes—"Sociology of the Body" and "Quebec Society 1"—had been reassigned to other professors, Dr. Travis Smith and Dr. David Webster.

The university’s defense of its actions often centered on the principle of academic freedom. Vice-Principal Andrew Webster described academic freedom as a "bedrock" of the institution, allowing for the investigation of difficult and distressing concepts. However, he also noted that such subjects must be framed in a way that is sensitive to the students and that academic freedom does not permit hate speech. The university has since proposed updates to its policies, such as including links to mental health and complaint resources directly in course syllabi, though this sparked debate within the university Senate regarding professorial autonomy.

External bodies also weighed in on the situation. Quebec’s Higher Education Ministry called the allegations "worrying" and "shocking," stating that while universities are places for debate, discriminatory comments cannot be tolerated. The ministry urged students to use official channels provided under the Academic Freedom Act to lodge formal complaints.

Throughout the unfolding events, Dr. Gosselin did not respond to requests for comment from media outlets or the student newspaper. The university administration eventually stated that she had no wish to comment and requested that she not be contacted further. The narrative remains one of a deep divide between a student body demanding immediate accountability and a university administration attempting to balance the legal requirements of tenure and privacy with the pedagogical necessity of a safe and inclusive environment. While the reassignment of her classes suggested a significant administrative intervention, the long-term status of Dr. Gosselin’s position and the outcome of formal complaints remain obscured by confidentiality protocols.
