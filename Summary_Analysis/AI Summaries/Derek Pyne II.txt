In July 2020, Thompson Rivers University (TRU) in Kamloops, British Columbia, issued a one-year suspension without pay or benefits to Derek Pyne, an economics professor. This disciplinary action marks the latest development in a protracted and public dispute between Pyne, the university administration, and the faculty union regarding the boundaries of academic freedom and workplace conduct.

The suspension was triggered by a Facebook post Pyne authored on June 10, 2020. In the post, Pyne praised the Brock University Faculty Association (BUFA) for its defense of Tomas Hudlicky, a chemistry professor at Brock University who had faced significant backlash and a retracted journal article after criticizing employment equity hiring practices. Pyne’s post contrasted BUFA’s stance with that of his own union, the Thompson Rivers University Faculty Association (TRUFA), suggesting that unlike BUFA, TRUFA and TRU were opposed to academic freedom. Pyne explicitly tagged the Federation of Post-Secondary Educators and several individual TRUFA members in the post, predicting that the tagged union officials would “go running” to Larry Phillips, the university’s executive director of human resources, to defend themselves.

Following the post, the university initiated a human resources investigation. According to Pyne, he was informed during a video-conference meeting with Phillips that his post had resulted in a harassment complaint. Two complainants, both members of the union, reportedly told the university that being tagged in the post caused them significant distress; one claimed to have lost sleep, while the other stated she required time off work to recover from the impact of the social media mention. In a subsequent letter from TRU President Brett Fairbairn, Pyne was informed of his year-long suspension and was immediately cut off from his university email account and access to campus resources.

The university administration has consistently maintained that the discipline imposed on Pyne is unrelated to his academic freedom. Darshan Lindsay, TRU’s director of executive and government relations, and President Fairbairn have both issued statements asserting that while the university fully supports the right of faculty to conduct research and criticize the institution, the matters involving Pyne pertain to "other issues that have arisen in the workplace." The university argued that progressive discipline is a standard procedure and that it is unlikely an employee would be suspended for a single social media post in isolation, suggesting a broader context of workplace conduct. However, citing privacy laws and employment regulations, the university has declined to provide specific details regarding the exact nature of the complaints or the history of the "progressive discipline" mentioned.

Pyne and his supporters view the situation through a different lens, framing the suspension as a direct attack on the right of a professor to criticize both their employer and their union. Pyne argued that he merely tagged individuals in a public forum without making derogatory comments about them, asserting that the harassment claims were a pretext for silencing his criticisms. This perspective is shared by the Society for Academic Freedom and Scholarship (SAFS). Mark Mercer, the president of SAFS, wrote to TRU leadership arguing that Pyne’s right to criticize the university and TRUFA is protected under Article 9.6 of the TRU Collective Agreement. Mercer contended that providing colleagues and union officials with the opportunity to know what a faculty member is thinking—even through social media tagging—cannot reasonably be classified as harassment. He further noted that the suspension severely impedes Pyne’s scholarly work by denying him access to his office and digital files.

This is not the first time Pyne has faced suspension at TRU. In 2018, he was suspended after publishing research in the *Journal of Scholarly Publishing* regarding "predatory journals." In that research, Pyne alleged that a significant number of professors in TRU’s School of Business and Economics had advanced their careers by publishing in illegitimate journals that bypass the peer-review process for a fee. Pyne claimed his 2018 suspension was a direct result of this research and his attempts to bring these findings to light during internal promotion processes.

While TRU also claimed the 2018 suspension was not about academic freedom, an independent investigation by the Canadian Association of University Teachers (CAUT) concluded otherwise. The CAUT report, released in November 2019, found that TRU’s administration had breached Pyne’s academic freedom and failed to protect his privacy. The investigation characterized the university as having a "broad institutional weakness" in its understanding of academic freedom, noting that the right to criticize one's institution and administration is a core component of the principle. CAUT recommended that the university review its human resources practices and remove constraints on Pyne’s speech.

The 2020 suspension has created a complex legal and labor situation. Although Pyne expressed skepticism toward his union, noting that union officials were the ones who filed the complaints against him, TRUFA eventually filed a grievance on his behalf. The grievance lists the violation of academic freedom as a primary ground for appeal, seeking Pyne’s reinstatement with full retroactive salary and benefits. This move surprised Pyne, who had previously felt that TRUFA and the university administration were working in concert to penalize his dissent. 

The ongoing conflict highlights a fundamental disagreement over the definition of academic freedom at TRU. While the university defines it as the freedom to research and debate respectfully within professional boundaries, Pyne and advocacy groups like SAFS and CAUT argue that it must include the protection of faculty members when they voice harsh criticisms of the institutions and unions that govern their professional lives. As of late 2020, the dispute remains unresolved, with Pyne facing a year of professional exile and the university standing by its disciplinary process as a matter of workplace safety and conduct.
