The events center around Dr. Michael Persinger, a renowned neuroscientist, researcher, and long-time professor of psychology at Laurentian University in Sudbury, Ontario. Dr. Persinger, who taught at the university for over four decades and won the TVO Best Lecturer Award in 2007, became the subject of controversy due to a "Statement of Understanding" form that he required students to sign during the first lecture of his first-year introductory psychology course. 

The document, which Dr. Persinger had utilized successfully for a decade without prior complaints, listed a sample of potentially offensive words that could be used during lectures. These included the F-word, sexual slang like "pussy," and homophobic slurs, alongside other emotional, silly, or complex terminology. The form asked students to acknowledge that the course would feature frank expressions and creative metaphors reflecting everyday life, and that hearing such language might cause emotional reactions. Dr. Persinger defended the practice, explaining that exposing students to emotional and profane words was a deliberate pedagogical technique designed to teach how emotion affects the brain’s rational processes and to encourage students to develop strategies so they would be minimally influenced by unpleasant experiences. Furthermore, the form stated that if the course content proved offensive, students could transfer to another section as early as possible. Dr. Persinger also noted that the course environment welcomed politically incorrect questions and open discussion, describing the method as a tremendous recruitment tool that helped foster independent thinking and critical analysis.

In December 2015, approximately two months into the course term, the university administration intervened following a student complaint regarding the waiver. Dr. Persinger was summoned to the office of Robert Kerr, the university's vice-president academic and provost, and was abruptly informed that he would no longer be permitted to teach the first-year psychology class. According to Dr. Persinger, the provost stated that the statement of understanding conflicted with senior administration policies, specifically respectful workplace policies, though no further details were provided despite requests. The university subsequently issued a statement noting that the dean had determined the form was not in compliance with school policies, leading to Dr. Persinger's removal from the course pending an investigation, with the chair of the psychology department taking over the year-long class. While Dr. Persinger was allowed to continue his other teaching duties, the removal sparked immediate institutional pushback.

The university's provost maintained that the core issue was not a matter of academic freedom, but rather that Dr. Persinger had improperly made the signing of a document a mandatory precondition for taking his course, arguing that admission requirements are never supposed to be decided independently by a professor. Regarding the obscene language, the provost stated that the administration would not have dealt with the issue had the document not been brought to light by a student complaint. 

Conversely, the Laurentian University Faculty Association (LUFA) and the Canadian Association of University Teachers (CAUT) strongly condemned the administration's actions. LUFA immediately filed a grievance on behalf of Dr. Persinger, asserting that the school had severely violated the professor's academic freedom—which encompasses the freedom of teaching, research, and discussion of controversial matters without reprisal or censorship. The union argued that such discussions are essential for fostering independent thinking and expression among students.

Dr. Michael Persinger passed away at the age of 73, prior to the final resolution of the dispute. However, the legal battle initiated by the faculty association continued posthumously and ultimately concluded in a victory for the union. In a consent award rendered on November 6 by arbitrator Kevin Burkett, it was officially concluded that the Laurentian University administration had indeed acted improperly and violated the late professor’s rights under the collective agreement by removing him from the classroom and issuing a related email to students without considering his academic freedom.

The precedent-setting legal outcome bound the university administration to follow proper consultative processes in the future prior to removing any professor from the classroom, which includes properly assessing academic responsibilities and ensuring the right to a full and proper investigation. Additionally, the settlement prohibited the university from ever again publicly inviting complaints against faculty members or utilizing a standalone telephone number specifically for complaints. 

Reflecting on the resolution, LUFA President Fabrice Colin expressed satisfaction that the administration recognized its failure to protect Dr. Persinger's academic freedoms during the mediation sessions. While the outcome was celebrated by the faculty association and national academic bodies as a vital victory for academic freedom across Canada, the union noted that it was a bittersweet moment given Dr. Persinger's passing. To honor his internationally recognized contributions to neuroscience and psychology, as well as his staunch defense of academic freedom, the university agreed as part of the resolution to establish a yearly scholarship awarded in Dr. Persinger's name.
