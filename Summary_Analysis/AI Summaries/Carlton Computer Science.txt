In March 2023, Carleton University's School of Computer Science advertised a confirmation-track appointment at the rank of Instructor, with a start date of July 1, 2023, and a closing date of March 31, 2023. The position posting, dated Wednesday, March 1, 2023, described the primary duty as teaching and engaging in scholarly activity, with specific teaching duties under Article 13.4 of the Collective Agreement including the delivery of three (3.0) full credits of courses in each academic year (Summer, Fall, Winter), supervision of C.S. Honours projects and B.C.S. Honours theses, and course preparation and course development. Service responsibilities included assisting with student recruitment into Bachelor in Computer Science programs, contributing to collegial academic governance through service on committees and other administrative duties, and any other administrative duties assigned by the Academic Head.

The School of Computer Science, which has 45 full-time faculty members and research strengths in areas such as big data analytics, computational geometry, computer security, databases, distributed computing, graphics, HCI, high-performance computing, machine learning, and networks, sought candidates with a Master’s in Computer Science (PhD preferred), a commitment to excellence in undergraduate teaching, and qualifications to teach core courses in systems programming, operating systems, software engineering, and web applications. Ability to teach advanced courses such as computer networks, computer vision, and functional programming was noted as an asset, as was industrial experience.

The advertisement included a statement that the School seeks to maintain its commitment to excellence and recognizes that increasing the diversity of its faculty supports this objective, and therefore especially encouraged applications from candidates from groups underrepresented in Computer Science and/or with experience in mentoring students from such groups. Application instructions required a teaching dossier including teaching philosophy, experience, methodology, and evaluations, plus a statement identifying strengths in advancing equity, diversity, and inclusion in the discipline and on campus, and future plans (maximum 2 pages). Applicants were also asked to describe experience with outreach, mentoring, research training, leadership and academic service roles, and any non-traditional research outputs, and to indicate whether they were legally eligible to work in Canada. At least three reference letters were required, and applications were to be submitted as a single PDF by March 31, 2023, for full consideration.

On March 6, 2023, Mark Mercer, PhD, President of the Society for Academic Freedom and Scholarship (SAFS), sent a letter to Michel Barbeau, Director and DSA Program Director of the School of Computer Science, responding to the job advertisement. In the letter, Mercer raised objections on behalf of SAFS, an organization dedicated to the defense of academic freedom and the merit principle in higher education. He argued that to maintain a commitment to excellence, a school should hire the person best qualified or most accomplished in the field, and that since diversity is unrelated to merit, attempting to increase diversity is not a sound way to foster excellence. He stated that the special encouragement of applicants from underrepresented groups suggested that the hiring committee would consider group membership a factor in an applicant’s favour, which he characterized as preferential hiring and a violation of the merit principle—the principle that academic decisions be made on academic grounds only. According to Mercer, favouring candidates based on non-academic characteristics disadvantages scholars for reasons unrelated to their academic accomplishments, abilities, or promise, and handicapping meritorious candidates cannot be a sound way to achieve academic excellence.

Mercer further argued that taking group membership into account in hiring harms individual scholars because others may view them in stereotypical ways and wrongly undervalue their work, and that seeking scholars for their race, ethnicity, or other identity forces them to suppress their dignity or decline an advantage. He also contended that recruitment based on a non-academic criterion undermines respect for the ideal of dispassionate inquiry, as summoning people by identity tends to confuse scholarship with advocacy and create consensus around dogma.

The letter also objected to the requirement that applicants submit a statement identifying their strengths in advancing equity, diversity, and inclusion. Mercer asserted that requiring applicants to an academic position to "swear fealty" to a particular conception of university community and university goals amounts to imposing a political or ideological criterion on hiring, contrary to university traditions of academic freedom and scholarly independence. He stated that demanding evidence of commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion ideology pressures academics into serving a social movement they might not support, encourages applicants to prevaricate or misrepresent their actual views, and can lead to self-deception. He also argued that such requirements are inconsistent with academic commitments to intrepid and dispassionate research, causing scholars to shy away from speaking positions that might appear at odds with EDI means or ends, and that the academic mission of the School of Computer Science would suffer either because promising researchers and teachers would be screened out of job competitions or because a chilling orthodoxy would envelop the department.

Mercer respectfully requested that Dr. Barbeau respond to the letter and noted that, with permission, SAFS would post the response along with the letter on its website. The letter was copied to several Carleton University officials, including the President and Vice-Chancellor, Provost and Vice-President (Academic), Deputy Provost, Vice-Provost and Associate Vice-President (Academic), Associate Vice-President of Equity and Inclusive Communities, and various other academic administrators. The job posting itself also contained additional information about Carleton University, emphasizing its location in Ottawa, its innovative teaching and research environment, its commitment to equity, diversity, and inclusion, and its accessibility policies, including the Paul Menton Centre for Students with Disabilities. It noted that all qualified candidates are encouraged to apply, but that Canadians and permanent residents would be given priority, and all positions are subject to budgetary approval.
