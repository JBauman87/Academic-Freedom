The Peter Wall Institute for Advanced Studies (PWIAS) is an independent research institute at the University of British Columbia (UBC), founded in 1996 following a major donation from Vancouver real estate developer Peter Wall. Its mandate is to support fundamental, interdisciplinary, curiosity-driven research and creative activities that have the potential to result in significant advances to knowledge, bringing together scholars across disciplines as an incubator for novel ideas.

In 2017, PWIAS Director Dr. Philippe Tortell, an oceanographer who had led the institute since May 2016, was asked by the Board of Trustees and the Vice-President Research to develop a new strategic plan for the Institute. He submitted a draft in May 2018 after consulting stakeholders, but the Board directed him to undertake a second attempt. After he submitted a revised draft in October 2018, the Board met in camera without Tortell or the two Wall Distinguished Professors present, despite their status as Official Observers.

On November 16, 2018, Tortell received a letter from UBC President Santa Ono, who was also Chair of the PWIAS Board of Trustees, outlining “feedback, recommendations, and requests” approved by the Board. The letter contained directives that Tortell described as requiring the elimination of a majority of PWIAS programs, including International Research Roundtables, the Distinguished Visiting Professor program, and the Wall Solutions Initiative. Remaining programs were to be modified to achieve closer alignment with existing UBC Research Excellence Clusters—networks of researchers formed under UBC’s strategic plan and overseen by the Vice-President Research and Innovation. Under the new plan, the Wall Scholars program would be significantly scaled back to five to six scholars per year, and scholars would be “expected to engage directly with relevant Research Excellence Clusters.” Thematic Groups funding would be awarded primarily to members of existing research clusters. Tortell stated that a large fraction of PWIAS funds would thus be used to support an ongoing UBC program run by the VP Research and VP Academic.

On November 20, 2018, Tortell resigned on the spot during a presentation to UBC’s Deans, Heads and Directors meeting. In his public resignation letter, he said the mandated re-alignment posed “an existential threat to the Institute’s core mission, academic independence, and capacity to catalyze truly innovative and creative research.” He called it “a dangerous precedent of senior administrators directing research at UBC.” He also described the UBC strategic plan as “an empty, hollow document” and called the PWIAS “a canary in the coal mine” for an overly bureaucratic and administrative approach to university governance.

Tortell’s resignation sparked an outcry from faculty. Dozens of UBC faculty expressed support on social media, with current Wall Scholar Evan Thompson criticizing the Board’s “top-down approach to research” and arguing that the best research is generated bottom-up by researchers themselves. The UBC Faculty Association and the Canadian Association of University Teachers became involved, with CAUT Council passing a unanimous motion to investigate potential violations of academic freedom and collegial governance.

On November 25, 2018, the PWIAS Board reversed course, at least temporarily. It announced that scholars would no longer be asked to align their work with existing research at UBC, and the Wall Scholar program would continue with its current criteria, review process, and funding for 2019. President Ono stated that the five-person board would consult with members of the institute on future decisions. Tortell called the changes “a welcome move” but said that making decisions from the top and then reversing them was not the right way to steer an institute.

The controversy led to multiple reviews. The UBC Senate mandated an External Review of the Wall Institute in December 2018, and a governance diagnostic review by a consultancy firm (the “Watson Report”) was commissioned in 2019 and released in March 2020. The External Review Report, rendered in January 2020 by four international scholars, recommended implementing governance reforms and addressed concerns about the president chairing the Board of Trustees and about the Deed of Trust.

In October 2021, the CAUT Ad Hoc Investigatory Committee released its report. It found that the requirement by the Board of Trustees and UBC administration that the PWIAS and its faculty align with existing research clusters under the Vice-President Research and Innovation constituted an infringement upon intramural academic freedom of these faculty, because the directive did not arise from collegial governing bodies containing a majority of academic staff. The committee also found that the structures governing the PWIAS internally and within UBC did not adhere to principles of collegial governance, and that the actions of the Board of Trustees in November 2018 were inconsistent with openness, transparency, and the primacy of academics in academic decision-making.

The investigation identified longstanding governance defects. The PWIAS Deed of Trust allows a five-member Board of Trustees, including donor family representatives and appointees of the donor, to make academic decisions, with non-academic trustees engaging in academic decision-making. The committee noted potential conflicts of interest, including President Ono serving simultaneously as University President, Chair of Senate, and Chair of the PWIAS Board of Trustees, and faculty trustees who had leadership roles in existing research clusters participating in decisions to align PWIAS with those clusters. Financial governance concerns included the holding of Wall Financial Corporation shares in ways that conflicted with UBC policy, trustee initiatives funding projects outside the PWIAS mandate, compensation to a donor family member for board service, and loans to the Wall Financial Corporation from the endowment.

The report also noted some recent developments toward improved governance: a presidential designate, Dr. Moura Quayle, was appointed to chair the Board of Trustees in September 2020; new academic trustees with defined terms were appointed; and an Academic Advisory Board was re-established in September 2020. However, the committee concluded that the resignation formed part of a history of failed governance at PWIAS and that urgent attention to governance by the Senate, Board of Governors, and the PWIAS itself was required to protect academic freedom and collegial governance.
